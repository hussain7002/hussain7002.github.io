## The context

The programme worked where maternal deaths are among the highest in India. In Assam's tea gardens, women pluck tea for long hours on low wages, often without the documents needed to claim maternity benefits. The tribal, forest and mining districts of Chhattisgarh and Odisha have poor roads and poor network coverage.

## My role

I was one of three members of PATH's management team, managing a four-partner delivery consortium of about 40 staff. I led monitoring, evaluation and learning, work plans, budgets and donor reporting, and analysed programme and portal data for each state government.

## Approach: solutions from the frontline

We formed core groups of frontline workers, mostly women, who defined the problems and designed the solutions. The best ideas were piloted in one village and then scaled statewide. In intervention areas:

- **Assam:** tea-garden workers got the documents they needed to claim the maternity wage-compensation scheme. Early antenatal care registration rose **25%**.
- **Chhattisgarh:** a checklist for frontline workers, combined with analysis of line-listed and portal data, raised the share of pregnancies identified as high-risk from **2% to 15%**.
- **Odisha:** a counselling tool for families encouraged delivery in facilities, and home deliveries fell by about **60%**.

The programme also brought emergency obstetric care training to the facility instead of pulling staff away, with a mentor network on call. It set up district task forces that put maternal health before district administrations, and supported the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare in establishing India's National Technical Advisory Group for maternal, newborn and child health at AIIMS, New Delhi.

## Outcome

The programme reached more than 500,000 women. When USAID's programmes in India were reviewed in 2025, it was one of only two projects retained. Strong implementation was one reason; disciplined outcome data showing that it worked was the other.
